Strategy

The venture's definition and its connected commercial choices: what the venture is, whom it serves, why customers choose it, how it earns, and what it advances next

Members and skills

venture-strategist

venture-definition, objectives, bet-portfolio, focus-plan, strategy-review; the coherence of the combined strategy

define-venture Establish or revise the venture-definition, the one plain-language account of what the venture is and does as a business (purpose and customers, what it provides, how it works, the commercial relationship, current scope). Use at the start of strategy work, when a choice changes what is sold, how it operates or whom the whole venture serves, and to reconcile the definition after commercial choices.

prioritize-bets Form, compare and select bets, each an intervention with an outcome mechanism toward a current objective, with its hardest assumption, the strongest alternative, a next commitment and continue/change/stop conditions. Use after objectives exist and commercial choices are available, and when a review or new evidence changes which bets deserve concentration.

review-strategy Diagnose what actual results and changed conditions mean for the current direction: check whether each bet ran with enough exposure, compare expected with observed, weigh competing explanations, recommend continue, increase, change, hold or close, and name the decisions to change at their owners. Use at a planned decision point, when a threshold is crossed, or when a consequential new observation arrives.

set-focus Turn the selected bets into a focus-plan of concrete next actions with real executors, timing (now, parallel, after, deferred) and actual prerequisites, pairing every blocked action with an enabling step so independent work starts now. Use after bets are chosen, when a dependency completes, when results or execution conflicts change the useful next action, or when work is stalled.

set-objectives Choose the dominant outcome and the smallest useful set of observable objectives, each with a target stated as an owner-set or proposed ambition with its reason, a horizon and trade-off rules. Use when strategy starts, when the mandate or stage changes, or when a review shows an objective is inadequate.

market-strategist

segments, target-segments, opportunities

choose-targets Choose which segments the venture concentrates on now, against the strongest alternative segment, for the current objective and horizon; write qualification rules that tell rejection from unknown, and the conditions that activate later segments. Use after segments and a first differentiation exist, when the objective or horizon changes, or when a review finds the target is wrong. Writes the target-segments document.

prioritize-opportunities Identify and rank the customer outcomes or problematic situations in the selected targets that the venture should address first, keeping evidence strength separate from attractiveness and naming the uncertainty that could change the order. Use after targets are chosen, when customer evidence changes, or when Product or the business model needs to know which problem to serve. Writes the opportunities document.

segment-market Divide a venture's possible customers into demand situations that change a commercial decision, with recognition criteria and an evidence-based commercial profile. Use when the venture needs segments for the first time, when customer or market evidence changes, or when a target, opportunity or offer decision needs a clearer customer distinction. Writes the segments document.

positioning-strategist

differentiation, positioning-decision, category-definition

choose-frame Choose the competitive frame in which target buyers understand the venture's leading advantage, test the expectations it creates against the actual offering, and decide whether category design is warranted. Use after differentiation is set or when the frame, offer scope or leading advantage changes. Writes positioning-decision.

define-differentiation Develop the venture's customer-relevant advantage against what target buyers actually use or do instead, as advantage chains with capability availability, support and trade-offs. Use when targets or candidate segments are set or change, when capability or alternatives change, or before choosing a frame. Writes differentiation.

design-category Develop a proposed market category when choose-frame selects or develops it as a live option, or when the owner asks for one. Define its distinction, test membership on included and excluded cases, and develop its name, point of view, language and adoption path through the frame comparison. Writes category-definition.

business-model-designer

revenue-model, offer-catalogue, price-book

design-offers Construct the purchasable offers - promised outcome, included scope and customer contribution, delivery status and path to first value, and any variant, entry offer, trial or guarantee with its purpose - and write the offer-catalogue candidate. Use when a revenue model exists or changed, when target opportunities or deliverable capabilities change, or when buyers do not understand or cannot adopt the current offer.

design-revenue-model Design how the venture captures value - who uses, benefits, decides and pays, which event triggers payment and what it obliges, the charging unit, timing, recurrence and expansion, and the economic drivers - and write the revenue-model candidate. Use when targets or opportunities are new or changed, when an offer needs an exchange to implement, or when observed buying behaviour or delivery cost contradicts the current model.

set-prices Recommend prices for each offer's exact entitlement, with unit, currency and payment timing, the commercial rules (discounts, anchors, tests, exceptions) within declared authority, and the economic basis and expected response, and write the price-book candidate. Use when offers are new or changed, when no price has been set, or when a purchase observation, cost or alternative price changes the basis of a price.

bet-portfolio · venture/{v}/strategy/bet-portfolio.md

The bets the venture runs to reach its objectives. A bet is an intervention with a proposed outcome mechanism (customer or operating condition → intervention → hypothesised response → objective contribution) that establishes, exploits or improves part of the commercial configuration. The portfolio states the chosen concentration, how bets reinforce or compete, the strongest forgone alternative, each bet's next commitment and the conditions for continuing, expanding, changing or stopping it. Operational tasks serve bets without each becoming a bet.

A diagnosis of what actual results and changed conditions mean for the current direction, and the changes it recommends at the decisions that own the causes. segments · venture/{v}/strategy/segments.md

The demand situations that justify different commercial decisions. A segment groups situations in which needs, triggers, alternatives, purchasing conditions or required delivery are similar enough to share a decision and differ from neighbouring groups enough to change one. A segment can be large without being actionable now. Target choice compares these segments; opportunity work and Evidence use their situations and boundaries.

differentiation · venture/{v}/strategy/differentiation.md

The customer-relevant advantage the venture has, or could build, over what the target would actually use or do without the offer. Each advantage is a chain: capability and its availability → comparison condition against an actual alternative → customer consequence → the buyer who values it most. Shared attributes can still be purchase requirements; a combination of common capabilities is an advantage when the combination has a meaningful consequence. choose-targets and choose-frame use the same chains; Meaning preserves their evidence status in messages; bets can prioritise building a planned advantage.

A proposed market category: the distinction existing categories fail to communicate, the membership that makes it a real category rather than the venture's feature list, the point of view and language that let buyers recognise and compare members, and the adoption path.
