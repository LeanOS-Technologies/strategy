# Commercial accessibility and target choice

Whether a segment can actually be won within the objective's horizon, and how to choose among segments that are attractive in different ways.

Consumers: [segment-market](../.claude/skills/segment-market/SKILL.md) (describing reach and purchasing per segment), [choose-targets](../.claude/skills/choose-targets/SKILL.md) (selecting the focus and its sequence).

## Accessibility is a chain, and the weakest link binds

A sale happens only when every link holds:

```
need is felt → a trigger makes it urgent → the decision-maker can be reached
→ they can buy (authority, money, procurement) → adoption fits their work
→ the venture can deliver the advantage → the purchase completes within the horizon
```

Strong pain does not compensate for an unreachable buyer, and easy reach does not compensate for a purchase nobody can approve. For each candidate segment, find the **binding condition**: the link most likely to fail or to take longest. The choice between segments is usually decided by their binding conditions, not by their attractiveness.

| Link | What to notice | Typical binding signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trigger | An event that makes the current alternative inadequate now | No trigger: the need is real but can wait indefinitely |
| Reachable decision-maker | Whether the person who decides can be contacted through a channel the venture has | The user can be reached, the buyer cannot |
| Ability to purchase | Authority, spending route, procurement steps | Purchase needs a committee, a contract review or next year's plan |
| Sales and adoption time | Trial length, number of people who must change behaviour | Trial over several jobs or a whole team must switch |
| Switching effort | Data, templates, habits, contracts tied to the alternative | Existing tool is mandated or embedded in a client relationship |
| Ability to deliver the advantage | The capability the segment values is available now | It needs a capability that is still on the roadmap |

## Horizon fit

The objective sets a horizon. A segment whose buying cycle exceeds it cannot contribute within that horizon, however attractive. Three moves are available:

1. **Sequence:** target a faster segment now, and prepare the slower one with enabling work that shortens its cycle later (references, a trial package, a missing capability).
2. **Bridge:** find a sub-situation of the slow segment that buys faster (one inspector inside a firm buying a single seat).
3. **Revisit the objective:** when the only worthwhile segment is slow, the horizon may be wrong; that is a question for set-objectives, stated as such.

Selecting on pain and size while ignoring purchase timing is the most common target-choice failure.

## Beachhead and scope

Geoffrey Moore's *Crossing the Chasm* (1991; revised 2014) describes concentrating on one niche whose members reference each other, then moving to adjacent niches (the "bowling pin" sequence). Used here as one pattern for sequencing, valid where buyers in a niche reference each other and where winning one niche lowers the cost of the next. It is not a compulsory model: a venture whose buyers do not talk to each other gains little from niche concentration.

A beachhead is a scoped choice inside the venture's accepted market breadth. The target-segments document narrows current focus; it never removes accepted product lines or customers from the venture-definition. A proposal to change the venture's scope is a separate proposed revision of the venture-definition.

## Qualification rules

Qualification turns the target into rules a person can apply to one prospect:

- Each rule uses an indicator someone can observe or ask about.
- Each rule states what happens when the indicator is unknown. Unknown is not rejection: a prospect whose firm size is not yet known stays open until asked.
- Qualifiers say who fits; disqualifiers say who cannot be served or cannot buy. A disqualifier that holds settles the case even when other indicators are unknown.
- Rules rest on the commercial mechanism (why a fitting prospect can buy and benefit), not on a claim that they predict conversion; that claim needs observed outcomes.

## Failure modes

| Failure | Signature | Correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attractiveness-only choice | Target picked on pain or size; purchase timing absent | Name each candidate's binding condition and compare those |
| Calendar expansion | "Move to segment 2 in Q3" | State the commercial or capability condition that activates it |
| Beachhead becomes identity | The venture-definition shrinks to the first niche | Keep the whole scope; mark the focus as current route |
| Silent unknowns | A prospect is disqualified because a fact was not collected | Add the "when unknown" treatment |
| Unreal alternative | Target preferred to a straw-man alternative | Compare with the strongest real alternative segment |

## Worked application

Illustrative fixture: fictional venture, not evidence.

The objective is "paying inspectors using the app on real jobs by the end of March" [owner: "Our first goal is paying inspectors using the app on real jobs by the end of March."]. Multi-inspector firms are attractive: several seats per purchase and a stated interest in team accounts. Their binding condition is the buying cycle: owners "decide slowly and want a trial on a few jobs first" [owner: "decide slowly and want a trial on a few jobs first"], and team accounts do not exist yet. Independent inspectors buy for themselves; their binding condition is reach, since the venture's known access is the three inspectors already using the app. The recommendation targets independent inspectors now because their purchase can complete inside the horizon, and sequences firms behind two enabling conditions: a defined trial on a few jobs, and team accounts. The reversing assumption is that firms decide faster than stated; one firm agreeing to a short trial would test it.
