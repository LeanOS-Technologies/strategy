# Alternatives and value mechanisms

Used by [define-differentiation](../.claude/skills/define-differentiation/SKILL.md) to decide which alternatives and advantages a `differentiation` holds, and by [choose-frame](../.claude/skills/choose-frame/SKILL.md) to decide which advantage leads the frame.

## The relationships

Positioning is a set of dependent judgments, not a list of attributes. April Dunford's sequence ([A Quickstart Guide to Positioning](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning); *Obviously Awesome*, 2019) orders them by dependency:

1. **Competitive alternatives:** what the customer would do if the offer did not exist.
2. **Differentiated capabilities:** what the venture has that those alternatives lack.
3. **Value:** what those capabilities make possible for the customer.
4. **Best-fit customers:** who cares most about that value.
5. **Market frame:** the context that makes the value obvious to them.

Each step depends on the one before. A change of alternative changes what counts as unique; a change of value changes who cares most. Used here: the dependency order, not a form to fill. The sequence is a practitioner method; it does not establish that any particular advantage will win.

## Alternatives: what the buyer would actually do

The alternative is defined by the buyer's situation, not by product categories. Ask, for a buyer in the target situation, what they do today when the problem occurs and what they would do next week if the offer vanished.

| Alternative kind | Recognise it by | Consequence for the comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Status quo / do nothing | The problem is tolerated or absorbed in existing work | The advantage must beat inertia and switching effort, not a competitor's feature set |
| Improvised tool (spreadsheet, word processor, paper) | The buyer assembled a workaround | Compare on the outcome the workaround produces and its hidden cost (time, errors, rework) |
| Hire or outsource | Someone else does the work | Compare control, speed, cost per job and quality consistency |
| Direct competitor | A product sold for the same job | Compare on conditions the buyer cares about, not feature counts |
| Adjacent product stretched to fit | A general tool used for this job | Compare fit to the specific job |

Discriminating test for a real alternative: a buyer in the target situation names it, uses it, or switching from it is the main purchase decision. A competitor that buyers never consider is left out even if it is famous. When most buyers improvise or do nothing, the status quo is the leading alternative, and comparing against a competitor's feature list is the wrong comparison.

## Advantage chains

An advantage is a chain, and each link can fail:

capability → operational consequence → customer outcome → who values it most

- **Capability** with its availability: available (documented or attested in owner material), planned (on the roadmap), unknown.
- **Operational consequence:** what changes in the buyer's work (a step disappears, an error class stops occurring).
- **Customer outcome:** why that matters to the buyer (finishes reports the same evening, fewer client callbacks).
- **Best-fit buyer:** the situation where the outcome weighs most.

A chain is broken when the consequence does not follow from the capability, when the outcome does not matter in the situation, or when the buyer named does not have the situation.

## Table stakes, differentiators and combinations

- **Table stakes:** attributes every credible alternative has. They do not differentiate, but missing one disqualifies. Record them as purchase requirements; do not lead with them.
- **Differentiator:** an attribute alternatives lack that produces a consequential outcome. A unique attribute without consequence is not an advantage.
- **Combination advantage:** individually common capabilities whose combination produces an outcome no alternative gives (data captured on site *and* assembled into the client's format without re-typing). State the combination as one capability and the consequence it creates.

## Comparison basis and quantified claims

A quantified advantage ("half the time") needs the alternative, the condition and an observation or justified estimate on comparable work. Without one, state the consequence qualitatively and label it `[hypothesis]`; name the observation that would quantify it (time three users' reports against their previous method). A persuasive chain is a mechanism, not a measurement.

## Trade-offs

Every real position gives something up: depth for breadth, speed for configurability, a desktop workflow for mobility. A visible sacrifice tells the frame and targets whom the advantage does not suit; hiding it produces expectations the offering will miss.

## Failure modes

| Failure | Signal | Correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feature-list differentiation | Advantages read as features with no outcome | Complete the chain or drop the row |
| Wrong alternative | Comparison with a competitor the target never considers | Re-derive alternatives from the target's situation |
| Planned capability leads | A roadmap item supports the leading value | Lead with an available chain; keep the planned one with its development need |
| Invented superiority | A percentage with no comparison basis | Relabel as hypothesis and name the observation |
| Everyone is the best fit | Best-fit buyer equals the whole market | Name the situation where the outcome weighs most |

## Worked application

Illustrative fixture: fictional venture, not evidence.

Brindlewick's owner brief says the inspector "records findings on a tablet, and the app assembles a client-ready report" and that "Photo sync from phones is planned for next year; it does not exist yet."

- Alternatives for independent inspectors: their own word-processor template filled in after the visit (an improvised tool, and the likely status quo for this buyer), and dedicated inspection-report products (direct competitors). Which dominates is unknown until Evidence reports it; both are recorded, the status quo labelled `[hypothesis]` as the most common.
- Leading chain: findings captured once on site (available, per the brief) → the report is assembled without retyping → the inspector delivers the report sooner after the visit → valued most by solo inspectors who write their own reports. The outcome is `[hypothesis]`; no time saving is quantified.
- Combination: the setup service converting "an inspector's existing report template into ours" plus the app means the client receives the inspector's familiar format; against a competitor product that imposes its own format, this is the differentiator for inspectors attached to their format.
- Planned: photo sync from phones stays `planned`, with the development need "photo sync built and demonstrated on real jobs". It informs bets; it does not lead the frame.
