# Objective trade-offs and outcome mechanisms

How to choose outcomes worth concentrating on, how a target differs from a forecast, how interventions are linked to outcomes, and how to read a gap between target and result. Consumers: [set-objectives](../.claude/skills/set-objectives/SKILL.md) chooses objectives and targets; [prioritize-bets](../.claude/skills/prioritize-bets/SKILL.md) links each bet to an objective through a mechanism; [review-strategy](../.claude/skills/review-strategy/SKILL.md) interprets gaps.

## 1. Outcome, activity, leading indicator

| Kind | What it is | Example (fixture) | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Outcome | A change in the world the venture exists to cause | Inspectors pay for and use the app on real jobs | An objective |
| Leading indicator | An earlier observable that moves before the outcome when the mechanism works | Inspectors completing a first report in a trial | A bet's control signal |
| Activity | Work the venture does | Publish three tutorials | An action in the focus-plan |

An objective written as an activity rewards doing the work whether or not it works. Test: could the objective be fully met while the venture is no better off? If yes, it is an activity.

## 2. Ends–means chains

An objective sits in a chain: mandate end → venture outcome → customer behaviour → intervention. Each link is a claim "if this, then that, because…". Writing the chain makes two things visible:

- **Which link is weakest.** That link becomes the hardest assumption of the bet that relies on it.
- **Which outcome is dominant.** When one outcome gates the others (no paying customers means no retention to improve), concentrate on it and defer the rest explicitly.

The smallest useful objective set is often one outcome plus, at most, one guard (an outcome that must not degrade while pursuing the first). Adding objectives to represent every group dilutes concentration.

## 3. Targets: ambition, not forecast

A target is a chosen level and horizon that expresses ambition and gives a decision point. It is not a prediction.

| Status | Means | Required beside it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| owner-set | The owner chose it | The quoted owner passage |
| proposed | This worker proposes it | Why this level and date: what it would demonstrate and the mechanism that could reach it |
| accepted | The decider accepted it | The acceptance |

Good targets are set where the answer changes a decision: "three paying inspectors by end of March" matters because it separates "some will pay" from "none will", which decides whether to continue the direct route. When no basis exists for a numeric level, an observation criterion ("the first paid use on a real job") is a valid target. A baseline is an observation or `unknown`; it is never back-filled from the target.

## 4. Trade-offs

Conflicts between outcomes are real when the same scarce input serves both (the same people, the same customers' attention, the same product scope) or when one outcome's mechanism harms the other (discounting to win customers erodes the price signal). Resolve each with a rule a later decision can apply: precedence ("paying use first; breadth of templates later"), a condition ("pursue firms only once one firm agrees to a trial"), or a guard ("no change that breaks the free pack").

## 5. Reading a gap

A gap between target and observation has several possible causes, and only some indict the objective:

| Pattern | Likely meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Intervention did not run, or ran briefly | Execution or exposure, not strategy |
| Ran fully; leading indicator moved; outcome did not | A later link of the chain (purchase, delivery, value) |
| Ran fully; leading indicator did not move | An early link (access, relevance, offer) |
| Target was set without a mechanism | The target itself is the defect |

A missed target is reported as missed. Moving its date is a new proposal with a reason, visible in the change.

## 6. Worked application

*Illustrative fixture: fictional venture, not evidence.* Brindlewick's owner material: "Our first goal is paying inspectors using the app on real jobs by the end of March." That is an outcome with an owner-set horizon; it names no number. A proposed numeric target ("3 paying inspectors") needs its own rationale: the smallest count that distinguishes repeatable paid adoption from goodwill among acquaintances, given that "three inspectors we know use it on their own jobs" already but "have not paid for it". The baseline of paying inspectors is 0 from the same owner material; the size of the regional market stays `unknown` because the owner says "We do not know how many independent inspectors there are in our region."
