# Venture coherence and value mechanisms

How the parts of a venture form one business, how to tell a coherent account from a list of parts, and how to recognise when a strategic choice changes what the venture is. Consumers: [define-venture](../.claude/skills/define-venture/SKILL.md) uses it to construct and revise the venture-definition; [review-strategy](../.claude/skills/review-strategy/SKILL.md) uses it to decide whether a result calls a single choice or the venture's whole account into question.

## Developing perspective into purpose and ambition

Recover the owner's observations about the world, their interpretation, the convictions they act on and the scale of change they intend. Quote the supporting material in the definition's Perspective and ambition basis line. These are governing inputs for development.

Develop the chain **observation → interpretation → principle → possibility → venture purpose → ambition**. Ask who could benefit if the principle were made practical, what they could accomplish and which available capabilities provide a first route. Construct promising applications beyond the existing offer, then connect the strongest possibilities into a coherent scope through the value chain below.

Express ambition through reach, adoption, authority or scale as appropriate to the owner's view. Choose the first commercial outcomes that serve that ambition, and carry both into objectives. Record actual capability and proposed scope in their appropriate fields so a first product can serve a wider purpose.

For example, the conviction that an inspector's judgment should shape their report can open own-format report tools, template conversion, teaching examples and shared professional practices. The purpose is to make that judgment usable from inspection to client understanding; the ambition can extend across independent practices and firms. A review tests the route and operating mechanism against results while preserving the owner's purpose; a consequential change of direction is prepared for the owner.

## 1. The value chain a definition must close

A business is intelligible when a reader can follow one closed chain:

**who starts in which situation → what they provide or do → what the venture does (and with what: people, AI, software, partners) → what they receive → the useful change → who pays, for what, and why that payment sustains the activity.**

Each link answers a question a consumer of the definition will ask:

| Link | Question it answers | Typical gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting situation | Whom is this for, and when do they need it? | A demographic label with no situation ("small businesses") |
| Customer contribution | What must the customer bring? | Hidden: the offer silently assumes data, time or skill the customer lacks |
| Venture activity | What does the venture actually do? | A capability adjective ("AI-powered") instead of an operation |
| Delivered result | What do they hold afterwards? | A feeling ("peace of mind") instead of a thing or state |
| Useful change | Why does that result matter to them? | Restates the result |
| Exchange | Who pays for what, at the model level? | Missing, or copied price detail that belongs to the price-book |

Test of closure: a reader who knows nothing else can say, in their own words, what the venture does on an ordinary day and why someone pays for it. If the reader can only repeat the venture's slogan, the chain is not closed.

## 2. Parts versus whole

A venture with several offerings is coherent when the parts share a mechanism, a customer, or an integration that creates value none of them creates alone. Three patterns recur:

- **One customer, several jobs.** Different offerings serve successive needs of the same buyer (a free template, a paid app, a setup service). The definition explains the sequence and why one leads to the next.
- **One capability, several markets.** The same operation serves different buyers. The definition explains what is common and what is adapted.
- **Integrated system.** Parts exchange outputs, so a larger package is worth more than its pieces. The definition names the actual connections, and only the supported ones.

When none of these holds, the "venture" may be several businesses under one name. Say so as a finding: the scope question belongs to the owner.

## 3. Status of each part

Every part of the definition has one status, from its source:

| Status | Source that establishes it |
| --- | --- |
| available | Product documentation, an actual release, or owner material stating it exists |
| accepted direction | An accepted Strategy decision or a mandate statement |
| proposed | A worker proposal awaiting decision |
| future | Owner material or roadmap naming it as later |
| excluded | Owner or mandate excluding it |

The most damaging coherence defect is a planned part described in present tense, because every consumer (messages, pages, offers) inherits the claim. The second is the reverse: an accepted line of business silently dropped because the current focus does not mention it.

## 4. Scope authority: focus inside the whole

A focused route (first target, first offer, first channel) is a choice inside the venture's accepted scope. It belongs in the definition as "the current focus is…", never as the definition itself. Changing what the venture is requires its own proposal with its own rationale:

| Change | Revises the venture-definition? |
| --- | --- |
| A new price or discount | No: the price-book owns it |
| A new first target segment within the stated customers | No: describe the current focus only |
| A new offering family, or dropping one | Yes |
| A different operating mechanism (service becomes software) | Yes |
| A different commercial relationship (sale becomes subscription access, or adds a service engagement) | Yes |
| Serving a different kind of customer than the venture states | Yes |

## 5. Reading results against the whole

A weak result usually indicts a single choice (a target, an offer, a route to buyers), not the venture's account. The account is in question only when results contradict a link of the value chain itself: customers do not have the starting situation assumed, cannot provide what the mechanism needs, or do not value the delivered result even when it is delivered well. review-strategy distinguishes these: "the right buyers did not see it" is an access problem; "buyers who used it did not value the result" questions the value link.

Strategy as an integrated set of choices that must fit each other and be supported by capabilities and systems is Roger L. Martin's framing ([Strategy](https://rogerlmartin.com/thought-pillars/strategy)); it is used here only to require that the definition and the specific choices agree.

## 6. Worked application

*Illustrative fixture: fictional venture, not evidence.* Brindlewick's owner material says the venture "makes report software for home inspectors", sells "a desktop report app", offers "a setup service", and that "The free template pack stays free." The closed chain: an independent inspector walks a property → records findings on a tablet → the app assembles a client-ready report → the inspector delivers it to the client without re-typing. The three offerings follow one customer through successive jobs: the template pack lets an inspector try the report format; the app produces reports; the setup service removes the cost of converting an existing template. "Photo sync from phones" is future, not available. If a later choice targets only multi-inspector firms first, the definition keeps independent inspectors in scope and records the firm route as the current focus.
